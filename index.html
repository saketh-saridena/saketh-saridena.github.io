Case study

Predictive maintenance for aluminum manufacturing

A classifier that reads 18 sensor measurements to predict bad decoating cycles before they happen, with a business cost analysis. Built for the CU Boulder DaSSA Hackathon.

Accuracy
94.4%
AUC-ROC
0.988
Recall on bad cycles
94%
Sensor features
18

Problem

Equipment failures cause unplanned downtime, and the hackathon's problem statement put each undetected bad cycle at $32,560 in losses. The goal was a classifier that predicts failures from sensor data with high recall, across 16,500+ production records.

Approach

  • Analyzed 16,504 records with 18 sensor measurements recorded at 5-minute intervals.
  • Handled severe class imbalance (about 85% good, 15% bad cycles) with SMOTE oversampling.
  • Used correlation analysis to find the strongest predictors: B_15 (0.655), B_16 (0.631) and B_19 (0.613).
  • Tuned with GridSearchCV and 3-fold cross-validation across 243 hyperparameter combinations, optimizing for recall.
  • Trained Random Forest and XGBoost classifiers, then ran a business impact cost analysis.

Results

  • 94.4% accuracy with 0.988 AUC-ROC.
  • 94% recall on failures, so nearly every failing cycle is caught.
  • Confusion matrix: 2,588 true negatives, 469 true positives, and only 29 missed failures out of 498.
  • Lets the plant schedule preventive maintenance instead of absorbing the cost of missed failures.

Tech stack

XGBoost, Random Forest, SMOTE, GridSearchCV, scikit-learn, pandas, seaborn, Matplotlib

US renewable energy forecasting

A machine learning project that analyzes US renewable energy production across solar, wind, hydro and biofuel sources from 1965 to 2021, with a forecasting exercise out to 2034.

Years of data, 1965–2021
57
Merged datasets
17
K-Means silhouette, k=2
0.881
Solar growth, 2010–2021
55x

Problem

Energy policy and infrastructure investment need long-term forecasts of renewable production. Understanding how solar, wind, hydro and biofuel production trend and move together matters for planning the transition to sustainable energy in the United States.

Approach

  • Collected US renewable energy production data through the EIA REST API and public energy datasets.
  • Explored the data: correlation heatmaps showed solar and wind rising together, stable hydro generation, and independent biofuel trends.
  • Applied unsupervised learning: K-Means (k = 2, 3, 4, compared by silhouette score), hierarchical clustering with dendrograms, and association rule mining.
  • Built supervised models: decision trees and Naive Bayes to categorize production levels.
  • Trained support vector regressors on trend-extended features as a forecasting exercise out to 2034, and built Tableau dashboards for the trends.

Results

  • Wind generation passed hydro in 2019, at 298.9 TWh against 285.5 TWh.
  • Solar grew 55x from 2010 to 2021, from 3.0 to 165.4 TWh.
  • K-Means separates the years into two eras, with a silhouette score of 0.881 at k = 2.
  • Solar and wind showed sharp recent rises with a strong positive correlation, which suggests shared policy effects.
  • Hydro and geothermal stayed stable, which makes them useful for baseload planning.
  • An 8-notebook pipeline covers the full lifecycle, from data wrangling to forecasting.

Tech stack

SVR, K-Means, decision trees, Naive Bayes, association rules, Tableau, EIA API, scikit-learn, Python, R

Case study

EZ Review, Amazon review sentiment analyzer

A full-stack web app that scrapes Amazon reviews, scores their sentiment with three model pipelines, and writes an AI summary for quick product evaluation.

Sentiment pipelines
3
Review pages scraped
10
Sentence AI summary
4

Problem

Shoppers face information overload when evaluating products. Reading hundreds of reviews is slow and inconsistent, and there was no simple way to get a data-driven sentiment breakdown with a summary of what customers actually say.

Approach

  • A React front end where you paste any Amazon product URL.
  • A Flask API extracts the product ASIN and scrapes up to 10 pages of reviews through Amazon's AJAX endpoint with BeautifulSoup.
  • Three sentiment pipelines: TextBlob (lexicon-based), DistilBERT fine-tuned on SST-2, and a hybrid where VADER decides clear-cut reviews and BERT decides the ambiguous ones.
  • OpenAI GPT-3.5-turbo-instruct writes a four-sentence summary of the common themes.
  • Results come back as JSON with sentiment percentages and the summary.

Results

  • The hybrid pipeline sends ambiguous language to BERT, where lexicon scores alone fall short.
  • Summaries pick out the themes customers mention most, such as quality, shipping and value.
  • One pipeline from product URL to insight.

Tech stack

Python, React, Flask, BERT, VADER, TextBlob, GPT-3.5, Hugging Face, BeautifulSoup, NLTK

Case study

WTI oil price forecasting

Time series forecasting of crude oil prices with six models, including deep learning LSTMs, and a 3-month recursive forward prediction.

Models compared
6
Best validation RMSE
1.287
Trading days
1,305
Forecast horizon
3 months

Problem

WTI crude oil prices are volatile and move with geopolitical events, supply and demand, and market sentiment. Accurate forecasts matter to energy companies, traders and policymakers. The goal was to benchmark several forecasting models on five years of daily prices and produce a 3-month forward forecast.

Approach

  • Collected 1,305 daily WTI price records (Nov 2020 – Nov 2025), reindexed to business days with linear interpolation for holidays.
  • Engineered 17 features: price lags (1, 5, 21 and 63 days), rolling means and standard deviations (7, 21 and 63-day windows), returns and calendar features.
  • Used a time-based split, 981 days for training and 261 for validation, so no future data leaks into training.
  • Benchmarked six models: a naive baseline, linear regression, random forest, LightGBM, a basic LSTM (21-day sequences) and an improved LSTM (60-day sequences with dropout and early stopping).
  • Generated a 3-month recursive forward forecast with the best deep learning model.

Results

  • The naive model had the best validation RMSE (1.287), which is consistent with WTI prices behaving like a random walk with strong autocorrelation.
  • The improved LSTM (RMSE 1.693) clearly beat the basic LSTM (2.314) thanks to a deeper architecture, dropout and early stopping.
  • Tree-based models (random forest 1.629, LightGBM 1.598) overfit the training data and underperformed on validation.
  • Direction prediction stayed hard, with every model near 50% accuracy, which reflects how random daily moves are.
  • The LSTM was chosen for the 3-month forecast for its ability to model long-term patterns and momentum shifts.

Tech stack

LSTM, LightGBM, random forest, TensorFlow, Keras, scikit-learn, pandas, NumPy, Matplotlib

Recipe recommendation system

A three-tier recommendation engine built on 20,000+ recipes with 680 features, with personalized suggestions based on nutrition, dietary preferences and ingredient similarity.

Recipes
20K+
Features
680

Problem

Finding healthy recipes that match personal dietary preferences, nutrition goals and the ingredients on hand is overwhelming, and existing platforms lack multi-factor filtering. The goal was a recommender that weighs nutrition profiles, taste preferences and dietary restrictions at the same time.

Approach

  • Curated and cleaned a dataset of 20,000+ recipes with 680 features, including nutrition, ingredients, ratings and preparation details.
  • Built three recommendation tiers: content-based filtering with cosine similarity on nutrition vectors, ingredient matching with personalized weighting, and hybrid scoring that combines both.
  • Applied dietary filters (vegetarian, vegan, gluten-free, keto and more) before scoring.
  • Used statistical testing to check that recommended recipes differ significantly from random picks on key nutrition metrics.
  • Engineered nutrition features: normalized macros, calorie density ratios and nutrient balance scores.

Results

  • Recommends recipes that match nutrition profiles with high cosine similarity scores.
  • The three tiers give varied suggestions, from nutrition-focused to ingredient-based to hybrid.
  • Dietary filtering keeps 100% compliance with user restrictions while keeping recommendation quality.
  • Statistical tests confirmed the recommendations are significantly more relevant than a random baseline (p < 0.05).

Tech stack

Cosine similarity, scikit-learn, pandas, NumPy, statistical testing, feature engineering, Python

Adaptive traffic signal control using real-time vehicle classification and weather data

An intelligent traffic control system that adjusts signal timing using deep learning vehicle classification, weather monitoring and a multi-factor priority index, and gives emergency vehicles priority.

Application number
202341042839
Claims
10
Core tech

Edge hardware

Problem

Conventional traffic signals use fixed timing that ignores real-time conditions, which causes congestion, wasted fuel and dangerous delays for emergency vehicles. Existing adaptive systems address single factors, such as weather or vehicle count, but none combined real-time video analytics, weather data, vehicle type classification and emergency priority in one system.

Approach

  • IP cameras at intersections feed real-time video to an edge AI control unit (NVIDIA Jetson AGX Orin, 2048-core Ampere GPU, 275 TOPS).
  • A deep learning module classifies pedestrians, emergency vehicles, two-wheelers, auto-rickshaws, cars and buses in real time.
  • A weather monitoring server with temperature, humidity and wind sensors gives two-wheelers priority in bad weather, for rider safety.
  • A multi-factor priority index per lane weighs vehicle types, counts, lane density, occupied area, weather severity and estimated passengers (car 3, bus 25, two-wheeler 1).
  • Emergency vehicle preemption detects approaching emergency vehicles and switches the signal or shortens the wait based on distance.
  • Hardware: LED signal controllers with Arduino and STM32 microcontrollers, CSI camera interfaces, and I2C, SPI and UART sensor buses.

Outcome

  • Filed with the Indian Patent Office (Application No. 202341042839) with 10 claims.
  • Answered four prior-art citations (US20210409649A1, US20190333375A1, US20220108607A1, US20160381325A1), none of which, alone or together, teach the multi-factor priority system.
  • Overcame a Section 3(k) objection ("computer program per se") by showing a tightly integrated hardware and software system with specific physical components.
  • The first system to combine real-time vehicle classification, weather-adaptive priority, emergency preemption and passenger-count estimation in one control architecture.

Tech stack

Deep learning, reinforcement learning, computer vision, NVIDIA Jetson, Arduino, STM32, edge AI, Python

Automated Monitoring System for Healthier Aquaculture Farming

A UAV-based deep learning system that uses YOLOv5 to detect dead and diseased fish in aquaculture ponds in real time, published at the IEEE ACCAI-2023 conference.

Detection accuracy
84%
YOLOv5 variants
5
Platform
UAV
Venue
ACCAI-2023

Problem

Aquaculture is one of India's key economic sectors, but farms lack automated ways to detect dead or diseased fish. Spotting them by hand across large ponds is slow and error-prone, and a single outbreak can devastate a pond within days, so early detection matters.

Approach

  • Built a custom fish detection dataset from photos taken in controlled conditions, with Google Images added for test diversity.
  • Trained and compared five YOLOv5 variants (nano, small, medium, large, extra-large) to balance speed and accuracy for aerial detection.
  • I implemented the YOLOv5m and YOLOv5l variants and led the literature review, idea formation and documentation.
  • Designed for drones: a camera flies over the ponds and images are processed in real time to pinpoint dead fish.
  • The system outputs bounding boxes with confidence scores so farm operators can find and remove diseased fish quickly.
  • All training ran on Google Colab with GPU acceleration.

Results

  • 84% detection accuracy for dead and diseased fish in aerial drone imagery.
  • The YOLOv5 large and medium variants gave the best balance of accuracy and inference speed for real-time drone use.
  • Published at IEEE ACCAI-2023, the International Conference on Advances in Computing, Communication and Applied Informatics.
  • A low-cost, software-only solution that works on any commercial drone with a camera.
  • Completed as a capstone at VIT-AP University, with a proposal, three review phases and a final report.

Tech stack

YOLOv5, computer vision, object detection, PyTorch, Python, Google Colab, UAV

AI Algorithms for Perovskite Solar Cells Fabrication

Research on applying machine learning and deep learning to discover, screen and optimize perovskite materials for next-generation solar cells, presented at PVSEC-31.

99.3%
Dimensionality, neural net
~90%

Viable candidates

Problem

Perovskite solar cells are among the most promising next-generation photovoltaic technologies, but finding stable, efficient compositions takes expensive, slow experimental synthesis, and density functional theory (DFT) calculations are computationally heavy. The field needed AI to screen thousands of candidates and predict their properties before synthesis.

Approach

  • Compared 10+ ML and deep learning algorithms across property identification, stability optimization and lead-free perovskite research.
  • Studied perovskites with the ABX3 structure, with models trained on DFT data (crystal structures, band gaps, elastic constants, decomposition energies).
  • Evaluated gradient boosting, SVM, kernel ridge regression, KNN, neural networks, logistic regression, decision trees, random forests, CNNs and transfer learning for material screening.
  • Screened 38,086 hybrid organic-inorganic perovskites with a GBR, SVM and KRR ensemble.
  • Applied SVC to 455 materials with 11 attributes for formability, and KRR, KNN and SVR trained on 354 DFT decomposition energies validated by experiment.
  • Extended the work to AI for memristors, luminescent materials and microfluidic synthesis.

Results

  • 99.3% prediction accuracy for solar cell fabrication parameter optimization.
  • About 90% accuracy with neural networks for crystal dimensionality classification, and about 86% with logistic regression for grain boundary identification.
  • Narrowed 38,086 hybrid perovskites down to 686 viable candidates for photovoltaics.
  • Identified 2,025 possible perovskites, 151 of them with band gaps suited to solar cells, using gradient boosting regression and classification.
  • Presented at PVSEC-31, the 31st International Photovoltaic Science and Engineering Conference.

Tech stack

Gradient boosting, SVM, KRR, KNN, neural networks, CNN, transfer learning, DFT data, scikit-learn, PyTorch, Python
