Case study

WTI oil price forecasting

WTI crude oil prices move with geopolitical events, supply and demand, and market sentiment. This project benchmarked six forecasting models on five years of daily prices, then produced a 3-month forward forecast.

Data
1,305 trading days, Nov 2020 to Nov 2025
Built with
Python, TensorFlow, Keras, LightGBM, scikit-learn
Best validation RMSE, naive baseline
1.287
Improved LSTM RMSE
1.693
Models compared
6
Engineered features
17

Approach

  • Reindexed 1,305 daily prices to business days, with linear interpolation for holidays.
  • Engineered 17 features: price lags of 1, 5, 21 and 63 days, rolling means and standard deviations over 7, 21 and 63 days, returns and calendar features.
  • Split by time, 981 days for training and 261 for validation, so no future data leaks into training.
  • Compared a naive baseline, linear regression, random forest, LightGBM, a basic LSTM on 21-day sequences, and an improved LSTM on 60-day sequences with dropout and early stopping.

Results

ModelValidation RMSE
Naive baseline1.287
LightGBM1.598
Random forest1.629
Improved LSTM1.693
Basic LSTM2.314
  • The naive baseline won, which is consistent with daily WTI prices behaving like a random walk with strong autocorrelation.
  • The tree models overfit the training data and fell behind on validation.
  • Every model predicted the direction of the next move about 50% of the time.
  • The improved LSTM produced the 3-month forward forecast. It did not beat the baseline in validation, so that forecast is an experiment, not a validated prediction.
